\begin{abstract}
Personalized split learning gives a mobile device two classifiers for each request. A device exit is trained for the user's own classes, and an edge exit is trained across the users of a cell. When users move, requests alternate between classes the device has learned and classes it has not, and the two exits are accurate on different parts of this traffic. Common split inference offloads a request when the device exit is uncertain, but a personalized exit is also confident on many classes it has not learned, so its entropy separates the two kinds of requests only weakly. We present DriftGate, which answers an offloaded request with a weighted average of the class probabilities of both exits. DriftGate corrects the edge exit for the class mix of its cell, sets the weight from how confident the two exits have been on the device's recent offloaded requests, and offloads only uncertain requests. We compare it with ten other inference rules on the same trained models in eight settings that include real mobility traces. Over the first simulated day, which includes early training, DriftGate has the highest mean accuracy in all eight settings, 0.1 to 1.9 percentage points above the strongest alternative. The margin is smaller once the models are trained. After round 30, DriftGate ranks first in six of the eight settings with margins of $-0.2$ to $+1.1$ points, and when trained models are replayed over a day of mobility it is 0.6 points above the strongest alternative on GeoLife traces but 0.3 points below a plain probability average in the commute scenario. \end{abstract}
